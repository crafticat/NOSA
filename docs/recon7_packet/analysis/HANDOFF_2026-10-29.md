## The picture in plain words

- **What we test.** "Speculative decoding" (spec) guesses 2 next tokens cheaply ("draft"), then checks the guesses and
  makes the next guess in one 4-row pass of the model ("joint pass"). If the guesses are right, one round gives about 2.9
  tokens per request. "SH" is plain cached NOSA in the same job: one model step, one token. "The strongest" is the best
  plain cached NOSA we have (whole-step CUDA graph, C 128 B 216): 3,426.2 tok/s on nova05, 3,410.1 on nova07.
- **Why it does not win here.** Our draft is the model itself, so each round computes about 6 rows (2 drafts + 4 checks)
  to keep about 2.9 tokens: **about 2 rows per kept token, where SH computes 1**. At B 216-318 the GPU is busy with
  arithmetic, so extra rows cost real time. Even the joint pass alone (3.1 SH steps) is longer than the time SH needs for
  the same 2.9 tokens. Free drafts would still give only 0.91x SH at C 128.
- **What the levers did.** Four "no new kernel" levers added +4 % (C 128) and +6 % (C 73) on top of I3. The rev-5 plan
  hoped for about +20 %.

## Measurement caveats (they do not change the verdict)

- **L60 usable memory.** After SH runs in the same process, only about 83.75 GB is usable, not 84.25 GB. The matched cell
  at C 73 B 351 hit out-of-memory for this reason; C 73's largest feasible batch is 318-350 (not measured).
- **L61 capture round.** The one-time CUDA-graph capture (6-7 s) lands in round 1, which the cell counts as a steady
  round. It moves the mean and the effective rate of the graph arms (mean -7 %); the median moves by at most one rank.
- **L62 first-arm penalty.** The first arm of every job pays about 4 s of first launches (fresh compile cache). This
  lowers that arm's effective rate only. So effective-rate comparisons inside one job favour the later arms.
- Smaller batches do not help in this code: B 8 gives 0.15x SH, B 64 gives 0.33-0.40x SH (the spec round is limited by
  Python launch time there).

## Two decisions for you

1. **The FAST tie rule.** Do you accept FAST paths whose only differences from SH are near-tie choices (I3 check cell at C 73
   B 100: 42 of 6,400 greedy tokens differ, largest score gap 0.3125)? If not, the result to report is the EXACT one (about 0.22x).
2. **The direction, given a ceiling below 1.0x.** Options (no recommendation from the measurements alone):
   (a) stop and write up the measured result and its cause (2 rows per kept token at a compute-busy batch);
   (b) try the four untested no-kernel items (draft-step graph, draft row through the existing fork, union lists once per
   pass, off-the-shelf compiler); best case about 0.58-0.62x;
   (c) allow new kernels; best case about 0.61-0.67x;
   (d) change the method itself, for example a much cheaper drafter than a full model step. Only this attacks the main
   cause.
